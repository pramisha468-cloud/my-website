THE FESTIVAL OF VICTORY

What is Dashain?

Nepal's much-loved festival of blessings, reunion, and new beginnings.

Why is it celebrated?

Dashain, also called Bada Dashain or Dussehra, celebrates the victory of good over evil. In Hindu tradition, it is associated with Goddess Durga's victory over Mahishasura and, in many traditions, Lord Rama's victory over Ravana.

Families gather, clean and decorate their homes, worship, receive tika and jamara from elders, share meals, and visit relatives. The festival reminds us to value courage, kindness, gratitude, and family.

FROM GHATASTHAPANA TO TIKA

The 10 Days of Dashain

The main days and their specialities. Customs can vary between families and communities.

DaySpeciality
1 GhatasthapanaA sacred kalash is established and jamara seeds are planted, marking the beginning of Dashain.
2 DwitiyaDaily worship continues, and the jamara is cared for as it grows.
3 TritiyaFamilies continue prayers and worship of the Goddess.
4 ChaturthiDevotion and household puja continue during the festival.
5 PanchamiFamilies continue the daily rituals and prepare for the important days ahead.
6 ShashthiWorship continues, and homes become busy with festival preparations.
7 PhulpatiSacred flowers, leaves, and other auspicious items are brought in as an important Dashain ritual.
8 Maha AshtamiGoddess Durga is worshipped with special prayers and offerings.
9 Maha NavamiSpecial worship is performed; in some traditions, tools, vehicles, or instruments are also honoured.
10 Vijaya DashamiElders put tika and jamara on younger family members and give blessings. Relatives visit one another.
Did you know? Dashain celebrations continue beyond the tenth day in many families, with Kojagrat Purnima marking the end of the wider festival period.
